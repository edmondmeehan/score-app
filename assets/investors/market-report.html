02The market

The state of dating apps

Dating apps are a $6 billion-plus global business that has stopped growing at the top and started splitting by intent.

Global dating app revenue passed $6 billion in 2024, with North America producing 50% of it (Business of Apps).

The category leader is flat. Match Group (Tinder, Hinge, Match.com) reported $3.49 billion of revenue for 2025, unchanged from 2024. Its paying users fell 5% to 14.2 million, while revenue per payer rose 5% to $20.09 a month.

The growth has moved to apps built around serious intent. Hinge, positioned as the app “designed to be deleted”, made $550 million in 2024, up 38%, and grew direct revenue 26% year over year in Q4 2025 (Match Group). Swipe-first apps are losing payers: fewer people pay, but those who stay pay more. Users are tired of volume and willing to pay for quality.

That is the opening for ScoreDate: a smaller, verified, intent-first product that charges for better matches rather than more of them.

03Five-year outlook

Close to $3B of new annual revenue by 2030

The market is expected to grow about 6 to 7% a year through 2030.

A separate forecast puts online dating services growth at 6.9% a year from 2025 to 2030 (Research and Markets). The demand base is broad: 30% of US adults have used a dating site or app, rising to 53% of adults under 30, and one in five partnered adults under 30 met their partner online (Pew Research Center).

Growth is shifting from more users to more revenue per user. Match Group’s revenue per payer rose 5% in 2025 while payers fell 5%, so the winners over the next five years will be the apps people pay more to use because the matches are better.

The gap is timing. Only 4% of singles discuss finances before a first meeting, and 56% wait until they have committed (Experian, 2024). By then, a mismatch is expensive to discover. ScoreDate moves that conversation to the start, gently and on the member’s terms.

05The signal

Why credit signals responsibility

A credit score is a track record of keeping promises, which is why lenders trust it with money and why it predicts relationships too.

How lenders use it

FICO Scores are used by 90% of top lenders to make billions of credit decisions a year (myFICO). A score from 300 to 850 sums up a person’s credit history: whether they pay on time, how much they owe, how long they have managed credit, and how often they take on new debt. Lenders use it to decide who gets a mortgage, a car loan, a credit card or a line of cash, and at what price. It is the most widely used shorthand for “will this person do what they said they would?”

What it predicts in relationships

Federal Reserve economists studied a large panel of anonymized credit records and found the same signal at work in love (Dokko, Li & Hayes, Federal Reserve Board, 2015):

  • People pair up with others whose credit scores are similar, even after accounting for income, education and other traits.
  • Singles with the lowest scores are about 30% less likely to form a relationship in a given year than those with the highest (Brookings).
  • Couples with the lowest average scores are two to three times more likely to separate than couples with the highest (Brookings).
  • The authors conclude a credit score also reveals “relationship skill and level of commitment”, not just money.

06Our position

ScoreDate’s position

The first dating app built on the finding that financial compatibility predicts whether a relationship lasts.

Credit bands, never scores

Members show a band (Building, Solid, Strong or Elite), confirmed with a soft check that never affects their credit. ScoreDate never stores an exact score or a credit report.

Matched on money values

Six questions (saver or spender, debt, who pays, joint or separate finances, five-year goals, investing style) feed the nightly Daily Drop, so people see shared values before they see a face.

Verified and invite-only

Every member verifies their ID and photos. Membership opens neighborhood by neighborhood, starting in New York City, for people 25 and older.

Built to get people off the app

Date planning, a safety share, calendar invites and post-date check-ins push matches toward real dates, the same intent-first model driving Hinge’s growth.

Premium by design

Membership ($24.99 a month), Premium ($49.99) and Reserve ($149, with a matchmaker) price ScoreDate above swipe apps, aimed at a user who already pays for quality.

The result is a self-selecting audience: people who are proud of how they handle money, and want a partner who is too.
